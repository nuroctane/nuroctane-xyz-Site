    01

    Orientation: what is a world model?

    Days 1 to 4

    Depends on: nothing · start here

    Lock the definition before the lineage. Four reads that triangulate the term: the founding artifact, a 2026 definitional roadmap, the position paper that split the field, and the field's own accounting of itself.

    • Day 1 · World Models · Ha and Schmidhuber (2018)

      The founding document. A VAE squeezes frames into latents, an MDN-RNN rolls them forward, a tiny linear controller acts: the whole loop trained almost entirely inside the model's own dream. The policy never sees a real frame until it already works.

      • The controller is deliberately tiny so gradients through the RNN stay cheap. Capacity belongs to the world model; the policy just reads it.
      • Every paper in the next 89 days descends from this architecture.
      Hook. 2018: two researchers teach a car to drive inside its own dream. 90 days of world models start here.

      arxiv.org/abs/1803.10122

    • Day 2 · A Definition and Roadmap for World Models (Jul 2026)

      2026's definitional ceasefire: world models as internal simulators that learn structure and dynamics, sorted against MBRL, generative video, and whatever LLMs do implicitly. Build a personal taxonomy here; every later paper files into it.

      • Watch what the chosen definition excludes. Definitional papers are battlefields.
      Hook. Ask five researchers what a world model is; you get six answers. A July 2026 paper tries to end the debate.

      arxiv.org/abs/2607.06401

    • Day 3 · A Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence · LeCun (2022)

      The position paper that split the field. Autoregressive generation is a dead end; the future is hierarchical JEPA predicting in representation space, with a separate configurator doing the steering. Half of units 5 to 9 is an argument with this document.

      • Score each 2022 claim against what actually shipped by 2026. The scorecard writes itself.
      Hook. LeCun in 2022: autoregressive world models are doomed. Four years later, half the frontier is autoregressive. What happened?

      openreview.net/forum?id=BZ5a1r-kVsf

    • Day 4 · Understanding World or Predicting Future? A Comprehensive Survey of World Models (2024)

      The field's self-accounting, built around one tension: is a world model for understanding (representation, control) or for predicting (video generation)? Skim wide, read the taxonomy close. This is the map for the next ten units.

      Hook. The world model wars reduce to one question: is the point to understand the world or to dream it?

      arxiv.org/abs/2411.14499

    Sources: worldmodels.github.io (run the demos) · Is Sora a World Simulator? (skim now; Day 42 in full)

    02

    Cognitive roots: minds that model

    Days 5 to 7

    Depends on: 01 definitions

    Craik called them "small-scale models of reality" in 1943. Three days grounding the engineering in the biology: how humans plan, what an agent society looks like, and why prediction and compression are one act.

    • Day 5 · People construct simplified mental representations to plan (2021)

      Human planners keep the smallest abstraction that still lands the plan. That is the cognitive license for every JEPA-style choice later in the series: throw away what the task does not need.

      • "Favor states that afford action" returns verbatim as the argument for latent prediction in unit 5.
      Hook. Your brain keeps the cheapest simulation that gets the plan right. Every good world model steals this trick.

      arxiv.org/abs/2105.06948

    • Day 6 · Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior (2023)

      Twenty-five LLM agents live in a text town and a society appears: memory streams, reflection, planning. A world model over social dynamics instead of pixels, and the observe-reflect-plan loop that agent frameworks still copy.

      • Reflection as scheduled consolidation: raw episodic memory distilled into reusable abstractions. Sleep, for agents.
      Hook. Before Genie dreamed platformers, Stanford agents dreamed a Valentine's party.

      arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442

    • Day 7 · Language Modeling Is Compression (2023)

      An LLM used as a lossless compressor beats gzip by orders of magnitude, on text and even images. Prediction, compression, and a model of the world's regularities: one object with three names.

      • Arithmetic coding over next-token distributions. Small code, big idea.
      Hook. A 14MB LLM compresses Wikipedia better than PNG compresses screenshots. Compression is world modeling, measured.

      arxiv.org/abs/2309.10668

    Sources: CitySim (urban-scale social world models) · Craik, The Nature of Explanation (1943)

    03

    Model-based RL: the load-bearing unit

    Days 8 to 16

    Depends on: 01 definitions · RL prereqs

    Nine days, trimmed to what still matters. From the policy-gradient substrate through latent planning, the Dreamer line, value equivalence as theory, and curiosity itself. Everything downstream assumes this unit cold.

    • Day 8 · Proximal Policy Optimization Algorithms (2017)

      The policy-optimization substrate under most world-model agents: DayDreamer, V-JEPA 2's fine-tunes, most WAM post-training. Read it as the baseline tax: clipped surrogate, GAE, and on-policy RL discarding every sample it collects. World models exist to stop that waste.

      Hook. PPO wastes every frame it touches. The next 80 days are about refusing to waste data.

      arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347

    • Day 9 · PlaNet: Learning Latent Dynamics for Planning from Pixels (2018)

      First to plan in learned latent space from raw pixels. The RSSM compresses each frame into a stochastic plus deterministic state; cross-entropy-method search plans straight over latents. No pixel decoder anywhere in the control loop.

      • Latent overshooting: train multi-step transitions, and long rollouts stay honest.
      Hook. PlaNet plans inside its own imagination and spends 2000x fewer frames doing it.

      arxiv.org/abs/1811.04551

    • Day 10 · Dream to Control + DreamerV2 (2019, 2020)

      PlaNet plus gradients: backpropagate reward through imagined latents and train an actor-critic entirely inside the world model. V2 changes one line, categorical latents instead of Gaussian, and doubles the score on the way to Atari SOTA with a fraction of the data.

      • Straight-through gradients over categorical samples, and why discrete latents resist posterior collapse.
      • The two-stream stochastic/deterministic latent comes from SLAC (in sources): what you can see, and what you cannot.
      Hook. Stop searching the dream; backprop through it. That change made Dreamer. V2's change is one line: make the latent discrete.

      arxiv.org/abs/1912.01603 · arxiv.org/abs/2010.02193

    • Day 11 · MuZero: Mastering Atari, Go, Chess and Shogi by Planning with a Learned Model (2019)

      A model that never reconstructs observations, and it beats AlphaZero anyway. It predicts reward, value, and policy; MCTS plans over those three heads. The idea was already sitting in Value Prediction Networks (2017, in sources). MuZero made it famous.

      • Model the quantities planning needs, and the pixels take care of themselves.
      Hook. MuZero mastered Go, chess, and shogi without ever learning what a board looks like.

      arxiv.org/abs/1911.08265

    • Day 12 · What Model Does MuZero Learn? (2023)

      The theory follow-up: value-equivalent models provably learn a compressed, control-sufficient slice of dynamics, and can lose observability when value targets underdetermine the world. Your first proof that world models are a theory field, homework included.

      • Value equivalence versus reconstruction: the lens for judging every latent model in this series.
      Hook. MuZero models what the world is worth. A 2023 paper works out exactly what that costs you.

      arxiv.org/abs/2306.00840

    • Day 13 · DeepMDP: Learning Continuous Latent Space Models for Representation Learning (2019)

      Learn your encoder by pretending to be a model-based agent: transition, reward, and discount losses, no reconstruction, no labels. The bridge from MBRL to self-supervised learning.

      • The trick returns in unit 5 wearing JEPA clothes.
      Hook. DeepMDP trains an encoder with three losses and zero labels. The trick returns in unit 5 as JEPA.

      arxiv.org/abs/1906.02736

    • Day 14 · DreamerV3: Mastering Diverse Domains through World Models (2023)

      One fixed hyperparameter set across 150+ tasks, from benchmarks to a real robot arm, and the first agent to mine diamonds in Minecraft without human data. Free bits, KL balancing, two-hot reward encoding: the practical lore, one ablation table.

      • Two-hot encoding: discretize the return distribution so one softmax covers reward magnitudes across orders of scale.
      Hook. One config, 150 games, Minecraft diamonds, no human data. The single best paper to hand an alien to explain model-based RL.

      arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104

    • Day 15 · EfficientZero: Mastering Atari Games with Limited Data (2021)

      MuZero's ideas meet consistency losses: self-supervised state consistency, value prefix prediction, and reanalysis push Atari-100k (two hours per game) to superhuman mean scores. Sample efficiency as a systems discipline.

      • Reanalysis: recompute targets with the latest model over old trajectories instead of collecting new data.
      Hook. Two hours of gameplay per title to superhuman. EfficientZero treats data like it is radioactive.

      arxiv.org/abs/2111.00210

    • Day 16 · Plan2Explore: Planning to Explore via Self-Supervised World Models (2020)

      Zero rewards, all curiosity: ensemble disagreement about imagined futures becomes intrinsic motivation, and the agent walks straight into the rooms where its world model is most ignorant. Downstream tasks then take a handful of gradient steps.

      • Disagreement as novelty: several heads on one trunk, cheap epistemic uncertainty.
      Hook. Plan2Explore explores wherever its imagination disagrees with itself. Novelty is ensemble disagreement.

      arxiv.org/abs/2005.05960

    Sources: Value Prediction Network · SLAC · EfficientZero v2 · dreamerv3 repo (run it)

    04

    Backbones: architectures for imagination

    Days 17 to 22

    Depends on: 03 Dreamer lineage

    What sits under the world model: transformers eating RSSM's lunch, MPC-native latents, denoising as an objective, objects as first-class citizens, and a minimalist audit that deletes everything.

    • Day 17 · IRIS: Transformers are Sample-Efficient World Models (2022)

      Delete the RNN: tokenize frames with a discrete VAE, run a GPT-style transformer over image-action tokens. Better Atari-100k sample efficiency than CNN-based world models, and the moment LLM architecture collided with this field.

      • O(n²) attention over the entire past, and it still wins. Architecture convergence starts here.
      Hook. 2022: someone asks "what if the world model is just GPT watching?" It worked.

      arxiv.org/abs/2209.00588

    • Day 18 · Facing Off World Model Backbones: RNNs, Transformers, and S4 (2023)

      Recurrent, transformer, and state-space backbones in the same harness. Memory capacity against sample efficiency against stability: pick your triangle before arguing about Mamba world models.

      • The phantom-transitions analysis shows exactly where each backbone's memory dies.
      Hook. RNN versus transformer versus S4 in one ring. The winner depends on how long the fight runs.

      arxiv.org/abs/2307.02064

    • Day 19 · TD-MPC2: Scalable, Robust World Models for Continuous Control (2023)

      The whole TD-MPC line in one stop: a decoder-free latent model trained jointly with TD-learning, planning locally over latents that never reconstructed a pixel. V2 scales it 80x into one 500M multi-task model covering 100+ continuous control tasks.

      • Short imagination horizons suffice because the value function carries the long horizon. TD amortizes the dream.
      • Task inference as Q-ensemble disagreement: read the task off what the value head cannot predict.
      Hook. TD-MPC2 is quietly the foundation model of control: one world model, 100 tasks, zero decoders.

      arxiv.org/abs/2310.16828

    • Day 20 · Denoised MDPs: Learning World Models Better Than the World Itself (2022)

      Train inverse and forward dynamics as a denoising autoencoder over latent transitions, and the model learns an abstraction cleaner than reality: nuisance noise cancels, structure stays. Theory included for when the denoised model beats the true one.

      Hook. Denoised MDPs learn world models better than the world. Train on what you can explain; ignore the rest.

      arxiv.org/abs/2206.15477

    • Day 21 · Better Slots, Better Worlds: Representation Quality and Robustness in Object-Centric World Models (Aug 2026)

      The 2026 edition of object-centric world models: slot representations decompose the scene, and representation quality directly predicts planning robustness. Action binding (2022, in sources) started this thread; this is where it landed.

      Hook. A broom action should never touch the TV latent. Object-centric world models are finally growing up.

      arxiv.org/abs/2608.12078

    • Day 22 · Simple, Good, Fast: Self-Supervised World Models Free of Baggage (2025)

      Delete the RNN, the transformer, the discrete latents, the decoder. What survives still competes on Atari-100k. Read it as an audit of which components are load-bearing and which are cargo cult.

      Hook. Delete everything. It still works. What exactly were we paying for?

      arxiv.org/abs/2506.02612

    Sources: TD-MPC · Binding Actions to Objects · Stochastic Latent Residual Video Prediction

    05

    JEPA: prediction without pixels

    Days 23 to 29

    Depends on: 03 latent dynamics · 01 LeCun position

    The other branch of the family: predict in representation space, never reconstruct. From I-JEPA through V-JEPA 2's robot planning to action-consistency diagnostics, closing with one driving application.

    • Day 23 · I-JEPA: Self-Supervised Learning from Images with a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (2023)

      LeCun's position paper turned into code: predict the representation of masked regions from context, in embedding space, with zero hand-crafted augmentations. The EMA target plus predictor is the whole machine; collapse is the whole danger.

      • EMA targets versus representation collapse: you will hit this bug in any JEPA you train.
      Hook. Contrastive learning needed 40 augmentations. I-JEPA needs one move: mask, then predict the embedding.

      arxiv.org/abs/2301.08243

    • Day 24 · V-JEPA: Revisiting Feature Prediction for Learning Visual Representations from Video (2024)

      I-JEPA at video scale: masked feature prediction over space-time blocks of internet video. Intuitive physics, object permanence included, shows up with zero labels and zero pixels generated.

      • Block masking in space-time: random rectangles in (x, y, t). Embarrassingly simple, weirdly effective.
      Hook. Never render a pixel and still learn physics. V-JEPA is the strongest evidence yet for LeCun's bet.

      arxiv.org/abs/2404.08471

    • Day 25 · V-JEPA 2: Self-Supervised Video Models Enable Understanding, Prediction and Planning (2025)

      Million-hour video scale plus an action-conditioned layer fine-tuned on 62 hours of robot data, then zero-shot planning on Franka arms in unseen environments. Watch video, control robot.

      • Energy-based planning: score candidate action chunks by predicted-state energy. No reward model at inference.
      Hook. The robot's entire embodied experience: 62 hours. Everything else is YouTube. It plans zero-shot anyway.

      arxiv.org/abs/2506.09985

    • Day 26 · UniJEPA: A Unified Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture for Task-Agnostic Visual World Modeling (Aug 2026)

      The JEPA zoo merges: masked prediction, autoregressive latent prediction, and action-conditioned variants under one architecture with one shared latent space. A consolidation paper, and a clean snapshot of where this branch stood in mid-2026.

      • Which variant wins under which evaluation: the comparison table is the paper.
      Hook. Masked JEPA, autoregressive JEPA, action JEPA: UniJEPA fuses the family back into one model.

      arxiv.org/abs/2608.07409

    • Day 27 · Diagnosing JEPA World Models with Action-Conditioned Predictive Consistency (Aug 2026)

      An August 2026 probe: do different actions actually produce different predicted futures? Consistency under intervention is cheap to measure and catches action-blind world models that every pixel metric passes.

      • Bolt this probe onto any latent world model you build or review.
      Hook. Your world model renders beautifully. Change the action; does anything happen? The probe says: often nothing.

      arxiv.org/abs/2608.12939

    • Day 28 · Is Forward Prediction Enough? Physical State Grounding for JEPA World Models (Aug 2026)

      The critique: latent forward prediction never forces physical grounding, and JEPAs can drift into predicting embedding statistics instead of dynamics. The remedy on offer: ground latents in physical state targets.

      • The failure catalogue: where JEPA latents quietly stop being about the world.
      Hook. JEPA world models can pass every benchmark while never learning mass or friction. Two recent papers catch them at it.

      arxiv.org/abs/2608.06799

    • Day 29 · Auto-JEPA: A Latent World Model of Continuous Intent for End-to-End Autonomous Driving (Jul 2026)

      JEPA thinking applied to driving, where dense prediction of pixels, BEV, and occupancy is the norm. Auto-JEPA argues planning needs none of that: predict a latent of continuous intent instead, and drive off it.

      Hook. Driving world models predict pixels nobody uses. Auto-JEPA predicts intent in latent space and just drives.

      arxiv.org/abs/2607.29031

    Sources: PhyLatent · SJEPA

    06

    Generative interactive environments and neural game engines

    Days 30 to 40

    Depends on: 04 transformers · 05 latent prediction

    The video world model era: environments learned from unlabeled video, then played in real time. Latent actions, diffusion game engines, a Nature paper, and the systems that made "world model" a consumer-facing phrase.

    • Day 30 · VPT: Video PreTraining, Learning to Act by Watching Unlabeled Online Videos (2022)

      The bootstrap behind this whole unit: contractors label about 2k hours, train an inverse dynamics model on it, then pseudo-label 70k hours of YouTube. Diamond tools in Minecraft from raw video.

      • Actions are easier to infer from adjacent frames than futures are. The asymmetry does all the work.
      Hook. No action labels at scale? Infer actions from before-and-after frames, then label YouTube yourself.

      arxiv.org/abs/2206.11795

    • Day 31 · Genie: Generative Interactive Environments (2024)

      All the way: zero action labels, ever. From 200k hours of platformer video, an unsupervised latent action model infers the controllable degrees of freedom, and the world is playable with a keyboard. Every paper in units 7, 9, and 11 cites this.

      • The latent action bottleneck separates what the player controls from what the world does on its own.
      Hook. Genie watched 200,000 hours of platformers and invented the controller. The action space emerged.

      arxiv.org/abs/2402.15391

    • Day 32 · Learning to Act without Actions (LAPO) (2023)

      Genie's idea pushed onto policy networks: pretrain an entire action head on action-free video by discovering a low-dimensional latent action space, then fine-tune the decoder on a few real demos for generalization.

      • Causal ordering: infer latent actions from a later window of the future, so they capture intent rather than appearance.
      Hook. LAPO pretrains a policy on videos that contain zero actions. The actions were there all along, hiding.

      arxiv.org/abs/2312.10812

    • Day 33 · GameNGen: Diffusion Models Are Real-Time Game Engines (2024)

      DOOM at 20+ fps, generated frame by frame on one TPU, an RL agent holding the mouse. The loudest statement of the neural game engine thesis: a game's simulation is its next-frame distribution.

      • Noise augmentation during training: inject noise into context frames so the model tolerates its own rollout errors at inference.
      • The drift analysis is the honest part of the paper. Read it.
      Hook. DOOM at 20fps. The engine is a diffusion model, the player is an RL agent, and nothing is rendered.

      arxiv.org/abs/2408.14837

    • Day 34 · DIAMOND: Diffusion for World Modeling (2024)

      Train the RL agent inside the diffusion world model, and it beats agents trained in crisp emulators: visual detail carries exploitable signal. Partial denoising from previous latents doubles as temporal memory.

      Hook. Agents trained in diffusion dreams score higher on real Atari. Detail is signal.

      arxiv.org/abs/2405.12399

    • Day 35 · WHAM: World and Human Action Models towards gameplay ideation · Microsoft, Nature (2025)

      The lab-grade version of the thesis, published in Nature. WHAM-1.6B trains on seven years of Bleeding Edge gameplay and generates minutes-long consistent sequences from a prompt frame plus controller actions, built for game designers rather than researchers. Weights on Hugging Face.

      • Read what breaks at the two-minute mark: consistency decay is the honest limit, stated by an industrial lab.
      Hook. Microsoft fed a model seven years of a fighting game and shipped the weights. Nature took it. Gameplay ideation is now a generative discipline.

      nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08600-3 · HF: microsoft/wham

    • Day 36 · Pandora: Towards General World Model with Natural Language Actions and Video States (2024)

      Video states, natural language actions. "The astronaut picks up the rock" is literally the action token. The everything-is-a-token-stream framing that world-action models still use.

      • Action-driven temporal attention: let action tokens gate which past frames matter.
      Hook. In Pandora, plain English is the controller interface for a generated world.

      arxiv.org/abs/2406.09455

    • Day 37 · GameGen-X: Interactive Open-world Game Video Generation (2024)

      Open-world scale: generate the game and control it, with instruction control over characters, camera, and environment. The endpoint of the neural-game-asset-pipeline thread.

      • Generation, interaction, and consistency trained as separate losses, then composed.
      Hook. Open-world games as a generative model with a controller attached. Who needs a studio?

      arxiv.org/abs/2411.00769

    • Day 38 · Matrix-Game: Interactive World Foundation Model (2025)

      The two-stage recipe the field converged on: huge unlabeled video pretraining, then action-labeled fine-tuning, with motion fields bridging coarse actions and pixel change. Then read DeepMind's Genie 3 announcement and study the gap between open recipes and frontier labs.

      • Motion fields as dense intermediate supervision: actions are too sparse to supervise pixels directly.
      Hook. Genie 3 shipped as a blog post. The open recipe (Matrix-Game) gets you 20 fps and 10 seconds. Study the gap.

      arxiv.org/abs/2506.18701 · Genie 3 blog

    • Day 39 · WanToFight: Real-Time Generative Game Engine for Multi-Player Combat Interaction (Jul 2026)

      Alibaba's Wan team puts a two-player King of Fighters '97 match inside a generative engine: real time, one keyboard per player, both controlling the generated match. Where GameNGen was a proof, this is product-shaped.

      Hook. Two humans, one generated KOF '97 match, at speed. The game-engine-as-model thesis, shipping from the Wan team.

      arxiv.org/abs/2607.12592

    • Day 40 · WorldCraft: From Camera Navigation to Object Manipulation in Interactive Video World Models (May 2026)

      Interactive world models mastered camera movement; WorldCraft adds object-level manipulation through entity tokens you can edit directly. The bridge from walkable dreams to usable simulators, and the on-ramp to unit 9.

      • Entity-decoupled representations keep world tokens and entity tokens separate so edits stay local.
      Hook. You can walk through a generated world. Can you move anything in it? Now you can.

      arxiv.org/abs/2605.25077

    08

    Text worlds: LLMs as implicit world models

    Days 51 to 58

    Depends on: 01 definitions · LM prereqs

    Do LLMs contain world models or statistics of worlds? The debate and its applications: Othello-GPT, probing methodology, planners bolted onto LLMs, and the quiet commercial branch, web agents.

    • Day 51 · Emergent World Representations: A Sequence Model Trained on a Synthetic Task · Othello-GPT (2023)

      A GPT trained only on move lists; linear probes recover the full board, and editing the probe changes the game. The cleanest existence proof that next-token prediction can induce world state, and ground zero for the memorization fight.

      • Probing finds representations; only causal edits prove they are used.
      Hook. GPT learned Othello from move sequences alone. Inside: a board. Edit the board; the game changes.

      arxiv.org/abs/2210.13382

    • Day 52 · Evaluating the World Model Implicit in a Generative Model (2024)

      The methodology paper: what would actually count as evidence of an implicit world model? Formal criteria applied to Othello and checkers GPTs, with the uncomfortable finding that chess-GPT's board is fragmentary.

      • Necessary versus sufficient probes: most headline results fail one of the two.
      Hook. Everyone says LLMs have world models. This paper decides what would count as evidence.

      arxiv.org/abs/2406.03689

    • Day 53 · Language Models Meet World Models: Embodied Experiences Enhance Language Models (2023)

      Train an LM on text generated by embodied agents acting in simulated homes, and physical planning improves over human-text-only baselines. Circular in the best way: simulated worlds produce the experience that grounds the model.

      • Wins come from curated successful action sequences; raw logs barely help.
      Hook. LLMs hallucinate physics because they have read about rooms but never lived in one. So: make them live.
